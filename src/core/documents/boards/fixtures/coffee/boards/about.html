Our Story

Named after a plant that asks for very little.

Little Fern Coffee opened in a narrow storefront on Hazel Lane with one espresso machine, a secondhand oven, and a fern on the windowsill that had survived three apartments and a lot of neglect. The shop turned out to need the same thing the fern did: a bit of light, a bit of water, and no one rushing it.

Slow mornings, on purpose

We could fit more tables in. We don't. Little Fern seats about twenty people, which means the espresso machine never has to shout over a line out the door, and the person steaming your milk can actually look up and say good morning.

We open early for the people who need coffee before anything else makes sense, and we close before dinner, because a coffee shop and bakery should feel like morning and early afternoon, not all day and all night.

The reading nook

In the corner by the front window: a green armchair that's a little too soft, a brass lamp that's always a touch too warm, and a shelf of paperbacks people have been quietly trading for years. Take a book. Leave a book. No sign-up sheet.

It's first-come, and it's usually taken by nine, but the rest of the room is yours — just pull a chair closer to the window.

What we care about

  • Small-batch beans — roasted forty minutes away, swapped seasonally.
  • Baked in-house — every pastry and loaf comes out of our own oven.
  • Compostable, not disposable-feeling — cups and boxes that break down, not just get thrown out.
  • A table for anyone — kids, laptops (off-peak), and dogs on the patio are all welcome.
